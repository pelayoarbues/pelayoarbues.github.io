## Highlights
- Since we’re on the topic of talks, I wanted to describe my process for generating the illustrations I use for my slides. Two years ago I primarily used royalty-free stock photography, but now I use images generated by AI (first via ChatGPT but now with Gemini). Over that time my workflow has evolved substantially, and I think it’s reached the point where others might find it interesting and useful. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m0646wnqywkww5aqjfkbfnff))
- Let’s begin with the big picture: I use AI to generate illustrations, not photos or diagrams. I don’t think AI-generated photos are appropriate for a presentation; at best they’re misleading and at worst they’re flat-out creepy. As for diagrams, I just don’t think that AI is (yet) up to the challenge. AI-produced diagrams tend to be overly complex, and while they look impressive, they tend not to aid comprehension of complex topics. And worse, they’re not editable: you can’t nudge a box or reword a label. So instead of just fixing the problem, you end up in a frustrating loop of prompt tweaking, trying to coax the image towards the diagram you could have drawn yourself much faster. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m0647vx0qpkzwnncht1bckqj))
- For me, illustrations are the sweet spot of AI-generated art. A good illustration doesn’t need to explain anything — it evokes a feeling, an image, or a memory in your audience’s mind. It supports your presentation, rather than overshadowing it, freeing the audience to focus on the most important thing: you and what you’re saying. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m0647zcx5vctga2s3mw4ndr7))
- For me, right now, [Google’s Gemini Image](https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-image/pro/) family (aka Nano Banana) is the best model for generating images. It’s widely available, high quality, reasonably fast, and reasonably cheap. While you can of course create images [using chat](https://gemini.google.com/), I think there’s a better way. A way that’s both reproducible and reusable, facilitated by a small R package called [bananarama](https://hadley.github.io/bananarama/). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m06489vdxnefbky97ar6qy01))
- You can’t generate art with AI without thinking about the artists. These models are trained on images scraped from the internet without artists’ consent, and the fact that I find them so useful doesn’t make that go away. I wish I had a tidy resolution; I don’t. Art is important to me, and I like to surround myself with beautiful things made by people. I don’t know if that feels like enough, and it didn’t feel right to talk about this workflow without touching on the uncomfortable parts. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m064dw5r76rtjyt72d41xx4b))
