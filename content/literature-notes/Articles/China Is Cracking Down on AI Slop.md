## Highlights
- China’s government might have some [lofty ambitions for AI](https://futurism.com/china-ai-deepseek-everywhere), but that doesn’t mean it’s going to let the tech run roughshod over the country.
  According [to](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/technology/artificial-intelligence/china-to-crack-down-on-ai-made-fake-images-and-videos) *[Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/technology/artificial-intelligence/china-to-crack-down-on-ai-made-fake-images-and-videos)*, the People’s Republic has recently begun tightening regulations around AI-generated misinformation, as part of what it’s calling the “[Clean Up the Internet: Rectifying the Abuse of AI Technology](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/tianjin/c105377/c7565797/content.shtml)” campaign. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ka9mmh21sfm83a84zx1zdrct))
- Like in [other countries](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/racist-sora-snap-welfare) unleashing AI, China has been plagued by a rise in AI-generated fake news, particularly in the wake of natural disasters. As *Nikkei* reports, authorities have been making examples out of a number of misinformation peddlers, including a person who shared AI-generated images of a [baby covered in debris](https://www.vishvasnews.com/english/ai-check/fact-check-ai-generated-image-of-a-baby-misrepresented-as-survivor-of-nepal-tibet-border-earthquake/) following an earthquake, and a 28-year old man who faked his daughter’s kidnapping. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ka9mmnsnq8qn1a8rk2kc8tpz))
- Taken together, the rules read like a shopping list of badly needed regulations in the US, where AI-generated misinformation and harmful images are nearly endemic across all social media, and where minors in particular have proven [especially vulnerable](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/character-ai-ban-minors) to harms caused by AI models. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ka9mn5x83vggqcex4299hvdn))
- The Chinese government has previously cracked down on [unlabeled AI-generated content](https://futurism.com/ai-generated-material-labeled-china), requiring all synthetic data to be explicitly identified as such.
  China joins governments [like the European Union](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai) in addressing the social consequences of AI by applying a comprehensive set of rules directly to tech companies. By doing so, authorities shift the burden of responsibility from the consumers of AI to the businesses creating it — an approach that’s [sorely needed](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/chatgpt-suicides-lawsuits) in other parts of the world. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ka9mnedfnyk215kz8v11ffh0))
