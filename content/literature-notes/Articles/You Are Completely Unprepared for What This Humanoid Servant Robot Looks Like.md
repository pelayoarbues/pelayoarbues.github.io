## Highlights
- Norwegian robotics startup 1X has shown off its latest humanoid robot, dubbed Neo Gamma, in a flashy new [promotional video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uVcBa6NXAbk) claiming to show the bot preparing tea, doing laundry, and vacuuming around the house. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jmwqsy107vbfj3k094yebz56))
- In a bid to separate its bipedal household laborer from the [far creepier competition](https://futurism.com/muscle-powered-robot-creepy), 1X wrapped Neo Gamma in what it's calling a KnitSuit, an eyebrow-raising onesie that's "soft to the touch and flexible for dynamic movements." ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jmwqt3s159cdh6aazt9zfhtk))
- It's a fascinating choice with some eerie results. Despite its full-body sweater, Neo Gamma's face is a more conventional panel of black plastic, dotted with an unsettling pair of set-back eyes. It's as if evil scientists crossed Baymax from Disney's "Big Hero 6" with Jason Voorhees, the hockey mask-donning antagonist from the "Friday The 13th" film series — with maybe a splash of Oogie Boogie from "The Nightmare Before Christmas" and the haunted sack guys from "9." ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jmwqtbc1sn67rxe8f2mv3khk))
- "There is a not-so-distant future where we all have our own robot helper at home, like Rosey the Robot or Baymax," said 1X CEO Bernt Børnich in a statement. "But for humanoid robots to truly integrate into everyday life, they must be developed alongside humans, not in isolation." ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jmwqtv6gwavskwtqjtd80czb))
- In other words, don't expect Neo Gamma to go on sale any time soon — although, as is typical in the hype-fueled tech sector, the company is simultaneously promising exactly that, even as it manages expectations. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jmwr0n3egm2m4hn554w8wwqm))
- 1X is far from the first company to show off a flashy humanoid robot designed to help out in the home. Elon Musk's Tesla, for instance, is working on its own bidepal assistant, dubbed [Optimus](https://futurism.com/the-byte/tesla-sped-up-video-optimus-robots). But despite plenty of fanfare, the EV maker has employed a lot of [smoke and mirrors](https://futurism.com/the-byte/tesla-robots-remotely-controlled-analyst) to make up for reality failing to live up to [some pretty bold claims](https://fortune.com/2025/01/30/elon-musk-reveals-massive-plans-tesla-optimus-self-driving-cars-humanoid-robots/) so far. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jmwr0yk0kxnetdr32w7famnn))
- California-based AI robotics company Figure has also [shown off an AI-powered humanoid](https://futurism.com/the-byte/humanoid-robot-powered-by-openai-scary) that can talk courtesy of OpenAI's large language models. The company claims on [its website](https://www.figure.ai/) that the second generation of its robot, Figure 02, is the "world’s first commercially-viable autonomous humanoid robot" — but has yet to announce price or availability. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jmwr146a5be22nwfmkvf1nmq))
- Interestingly, 1X also [received funding from OpenAI last year](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/11/openai-backed-1x-raises-another-100m-for-the-race-to-humanoid-robots/) as part of a $100 million series, in another sign of the hype for humanoid robots that can talk to their masters with the help of generative AI. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jmwr16km4b1abbg6aap8s5h4))
- But despite the attention and investments being poured into the industry, nobody really knows when — or if — we'll see robots like Neo Gamma being offered to consumers. The engineering challenges are immense, and whether they can prove to be actually useful in a home setting, let alone be affordable to those who aren't hugely wealthy, remains to be seen. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jmwr1ekzjy2jjfmnnwgbswsq))
