## Highlights
- While Large Language Models (LLMs) like LLama 2 have shown remarkable prowess in understanding and generating text, they have a critical limitation: They can only answer questions based on single portions of text. This limitation becomes most apparent when dealing with documents that include contradictions or when the context spans multiple sections. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kgtcwtgjw047rwb67gx2r6n5))
- But there is something that I believe will allow LLMs to become accurate at scale: Knowledge Graphs. In a nutshell, Knowledge Graphs serve as a structured repository of facts and relationships, providing contextual interconnected data LLMs need to give accurate answers to prompts. Essentially, they help LLMs understand what they are actually saying. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kgtcwx680bhvfeswt12b8eez))
- There's an ongoing debate about whether LLMs truly understand what they're processing. My view is that they don’t. They’re not like a child learning to avoid putting their hand in the fire, where only one warning or learning episode is enough to embed the understanding in their mind. With LLMs, it's a more complex challenge that demands repetitive training without much information as to when the understanding finally comes. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kgtcx0xkfcm6amahff78s6x9))
- That lack of understanding of context, especially when it spans multiple domains or requires reasoning, isn’t sustainable, especially when dealing with complex and contradictory information. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kgtcx2s2wc6ge7pnpypbbg9j))
- For instance, imagine a lawyer reviewing a software contract to check that user data is stored in the European Union to comply with GDPR laws. The contract mentions that it is, but in another section, it also mentions an external cloud provider located in the U.S. Now, if you ask whether this scenario is compliant, a standard language model might get confused. It might see the requirement for EU storage and assume compliance, or notice the U.S. location and think it's not. Equally, it might not know whether the U.S. is part of the EU or not. What it struggles with is connecting these two pieces of information to understand that using external providers to store data in the U.S. would actually violate the GDPR requirement, despite the other provision. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kgtcx4m58cszgbswr83vrpam))
- Enter Knowledge Graphs: They contribute to an LLM’s ability to grasp context by providing a framework that mirrors human cognition, where context is derived from a network of concepts and experiences. They connect the different dots together and come up with a response that uses information stored in different locations. This enables LLMs to generate responses that are not only relevant but also enriched with the nuance and depth provided by the knowledge graph’s structured data. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kgtcx6m4b0pb2td87wx8ygxj))
- LLMs can also generate incorrect or outdated information because they rely on the data available during training. For example, Chat GPT 4 turbo has a cut-off date of [April 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/06/openai-announces-more-powerful-gpt-4-turbo-and-cuts-prices.html#:~:text=GPT%2D4%20Turbo%20is%20the,GPT%2D4%20Turbo%20can.). Knowledge graphs come into play by serving as a dynamic, up-to-date database that LLMs can query. Integrating real-time data from knowledge graphs ensures that LLMs have access to the latest facts, which is crucial in fields like medicine or law where accuracy is business-critical. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kgtcxb5c622q130k54f0w5fk))
- Specialization is another area where LLMs can benefit from knowledge graphs. By tapping into industry-specific knowledge graphs, LLMs can provide expert-level insights in niche domains. This is particularly useful for businesses that require a deep understanding of a particular field. Knowledge graphs enable customization by aligning the LLM’s outputs with the user’s historical data and preferences. This tailoring can make interactions with LLMs feel more personal and relevant. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kgtcxekhxe32dahn5nrpdwde))
- Finally, incorporating knowledge graphs with LLMs can also make their use more reliably ethical by providing a structured framework that guides the LLM's outputs to align with predetermined standards. For instance, by setting parameters that define age-appropriate content, a knowledge graph can ensure that an LLM consistently adheres to PG-13 guidelines. Similarly, in customer support scenarios, a knowledge graph tailored to a company's policies and help centre resources can prevent misuse and maintain the integrity of the support provided. Overall, knowledge graphs can act as a foundation for responsible and controlled LLM behavior, based on specific organizational values and ethical considerations. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kgtcxjwmv9jn8z5m6rzyh63t))
- Both LLMs and knowledge graphs are complex, state-of-the-art technologies, and linking them within a single application is a major challenge. The key issue is ensuring effective communication and coordination between these two systems, which can be quite complex to deploy individually. When attempting to combine them, the complexity increases, making the integration process even harder. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kgtcxnj6rarnaz64mbv1yf9k))
- For most businesses, investing in proprietary LLMs will likely be unnecessary unless your core offerings depend on customized, LLM-driven solutions. So while it will be important to understand *whether* you need Knowledge Graphs—primarily by understanding your business case for them and whether your LLM has knowledge gaps that contextual grounding can solve—chances are that the continued evolution of LLM integration tools will mean you don’t need to build them yourself. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kgtcxxa73fkbac7k00jm1wkd))
