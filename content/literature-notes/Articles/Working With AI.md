## Highlights
- How are businesses across the economy adopting AI? What bottlenecks and challenges have they faced? How can employers reorganize human expertise around increasingly capable models? To understand businesses’ experiences better, we conducted 66 interviews with 58 of Anthropic’s enterprise customers. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86hrzzkhydc0ky2d70a4f0f))
- **Sources of AI value.** Companies are deploying AI across a dizzying range of knowledge work tasks. In particular, they are increasingly using coding agents to bring software capabilities to nontechnical roles. Interviewees report that most uses of AI add value by increasing their output, rather than by cutting costs. They attribute this both to AI accelerating *existing* work and to its role in introducing novel product and service improvements. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86hs1sfb139rps97e5ysgpw))
- **Impact on employment.** Across our interviews, we saw very few examples of domestic layoffs that were driven by AI automation. Although some companies reported hiring more due to AI, most companies reported hiring less due to AI. Many also reported reducing their use of contractors, particularly offshore ones. These reported cuts could be an early warning of job disruption in the international business services industry. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86hsxekdgsw2x7t725jxjkk))
- **Bottlenecks.** Companies reported a range of bottlenecks, including experts resisting AI deployment and tasks that are not accelerated by AI. As coding speeds up, for example, other tasks—like code review, sales, and strategic decision-making—increasingly need to keep pace. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86ht1pmrwsfywhrtzjsr22k))
- **Limits to top-down and bottom-up approaches.** Many companies approach AI by identifying a workflow to automate and rolling out a top-down solution. This approach can lead to job insecurity, fears of workplace surveillance, and resistance from the experts needed to set up an effective and durable workflow. Companies that distribute AI tools and wait for bottom-up adoption see some valuable AI use but face bottlenecks from unequal adoption and single-task AI implementations. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86ht54s7h7zesqs2afwdkzb))
- **Reorganizing for AI.** Interviewees described the highest-value uses as involving meaningful reorganization. Some companies are broadening their employees’ roles to ensure individuals can take ownership over whole workflows, and are redeploying employees to the “bottleneck” tasks that AI models cannot do. Some also seek to empower employees by building out infrastructure to support nondeveloper coding and by providing broad-based internal access to business data. These companies are reorganizing to take advantage of ongoing model capability improvements rather than settling on a fixed approach that assumes AI tools will remain as powerful as they are today. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m86htbhvabxwdm3ykah45ah6))
