## Highlights
- Throughout this series, I've carefully avoided diving into the elephant in the room: Generative AI. This wasn't by accident. I wanted to establish the fundamental principles of building AI products before tackling how GenAI is (and isn't) changing the game. Because here's the thing: while the media frenzy around GenAI might make you think everything we knew about building AI products has gone out the window, the reality is more nuanced. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp87vc0nwhd41r28ghsvg7xg))
- Remember in Spinal Tap when guitarist Nigel Tufnel shows off his amplifier that goes up to eleven instead of the standard ten? "It's one louder," he explains with complete conviction. That scene keeps coming to mind as I try to make sense of our GenAI journey at BBVA over the past 24 months. Everything we knew about building AI products still applies, but somehow it all got... turned up to eleven. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp87vz49zz7sk7hxts84v7ym))
- Our GenAI story at BBVA began earlier than most. By early 2022, thanks to some insistent internal voices (I should have listened to you earlier!) and our ongoing dialogue with Dario Gil (from IBM Research) and Kai Fu Lee (from Sinovation Ventures), we recognized the technology's potential and made a big bet: we proposed creating a dedicated Language group. This wasn't a small investment: it required approval from the bank's highest leadership. But we were convinced that language technology would be transformative for banking, and our leadership backed that vision. We felt pretty clever about it too; we thought we had two, maybe three years to prepare. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp87wbyfyyh9w11xdvcz59ca))
- Fast forward to today: what started as a core team exploring the technology has evolved into a full-fledged organization managing roughly 35 GenAI projects, with several already in Production and others in friends & family testing. In addition to those top down initiatives, we have an additional 800+ use cases that have been prototyped bottom up by the 3300 employees (and counting…) using ChatGPT Enterprise, a story that was actually profiled both by [OpenAI](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https%3A%2F%2Fopenai%2Ecom%2Findex%2Fbbva%2F&urlhash=DJ-D&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) and in the [WSJ](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww%2Ewsj%2Ecom%2Farticles%2Fsix-months-thousands-of-gpts-and-some-big-unknowns-inside-openais-deal-with-bbva-5d6f1c03&urlhash=s0NP&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) a couple of weeks ago. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp87x6jx5rkj5fbgvae4qpd3))
- And unless you’ve been living under a rock you know that this explosion isn't unique to us. According to the latest Forrester Q2 AI Pulse Survey (2024), the number of new GenAI projects nearly matches those using traditional predictive models. Mind-blowing doesn't begin to cover it. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp87xdh56wftqkbd1ee6wcw2))
- With 35 serious projects and hundreds of employee-led experiments under our belt, you'd think we'd have this all figured out. After all, GenAI projects seem simpler than traditional AI initiatives on paper. You're not building the model yourself (you couldn't even if you wanted to). You don't need to source training data. You don't worry about infrastructure or downtime. No more labeling. And the service gets cheaper incredibly fast (OpenAI's GPT-4 API has seen a 90% [price drop](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww%2Enebuly%2Ecom%2Fblog%2Fopenai-gpt-4-api-pricing&urlhash=BeGN&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) in 16 months). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp87xtb732266h0ed98psprk))
- This apparent simplicity is deceptive. While many of our employees are successfully using ChatGPT Enterprise to solve business needs, our journey with those 35 projects that require deep integration into our systems and processes has taught us something different: when you need to build enterprise-grade GenAI solutions, what looks like plug-and-play technology actually requires fundamentally rethinking our entire approach to system design. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp87y6zvj9vpt94zanykc8aj))
- The first idea is a subtle but crucial one: the power of these systems often tempts us to build point solutions rather than system solutions. If you've read "[Power & Prediction](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww%2Eavigoldfarb%2Ecom%2Fpowerandprediction&urlhash=FEhO&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)" by Agrawal and Goldfarb, this might sound familiar. They make a compelling case that the true value of new technologies emerges when we redesign entire systems around their capabilities, not when we simply plug them into existing processes. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp87z5stgveewey9h1cy3y6b))
- The ability to understand and generate human language is so novel and powerful that it's easy to fall into the trap of building "nice-to-have" features rather than “must-have” solutions. We've seen this repeatedly: teams get excited about automating a small part of a process (like document review) because the AI can now understand the text. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8826aqh1hh2302qaxg4wn4))
- But here's the thing: just changing one part of a process rarely delivers transformative results. The real power of GenAI emerges when you reengineer entire processes to exploit its capabilities. As Agrawal and Goldfarb would put it, we're still in the "point solution" phase with most GenAI implementations; making existing processes marginally better instead of reimagining them from the ground up. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp882baq58mabm83kx669ghz))
- This is especially challenging when dealing with processes that already work well. It's a classic example of the innovator's dilemma at play: the existing process is "good enough," making it hard to justify the effort and risk of systematic change, even when the potential benefits are substantial. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp882jafyfy7xtk1jk9vqqsh))
- This insight drove us to fundamentally reshape how we organize these projects at BBVA. Instead of using our standard AI/ML project structure, we created dedicated value streams led by senior business leaders with strong technical understanding. This wasn't just an org chart shuffle; it was a recognition that GenAI's system-wide implications demanded a more holistic approach. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp882m9k5v7y1nnkkcvtfm78))
- Here's something nobody warns you about: while you don't need training data, you absolutely need a solid knowledge base to make these systems useful. And guess what? Many of us, including banks, have struggled with managing unstructured data. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8831r4f1q3qgxnjcas5zhn))
- We discovered this the hard way when improving our Virtual Assistant. The FAQ database it relied on hadn't been updated with this kind of application in mind, and connecting it from its legacy system to our new architecture proved quite a challenge. The new "labeling" isn't about training data. It's about maintaining and governing your knowledge bases. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp883qty0v08emvvvra8y17a))
- Try building one of these systems and you'll quickly run into a few headaches:
  • Unstructured data has been the forgotten child of data governance programs.
  • Knowledge bases need constant attention: leave them alone for a while and they quickly become a mess.
  • Quality control? That's a whole new beast. How do you even check if your knowledge base is "good enough"?
  • The truth is... complicated. Different teams (sometimes even different people!) maintain their own versions of what's "correct". ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp883w610f2nyd180te7342y))
- The irony? While LLMs reduce the need for labeled training data, they've created an entirely new data management challenge that many organizations aren't prepared for. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8b90etdk3c1hk7q0qh6zbn))
- Remember when I said start with integrations? Well, with GenAI, that advice becomes both more important and more complicated. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8b95qgmtw5nw1evs5pfrfc))
- Building GenAI systems means orchestrating multiple components, and each one brings its own headaches. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8b99s8wkry7n1te4f599ak))
- First, as you know now, you need somewhere to store and manage your knowledge base. You need vector databases for RAG (yeah, those fancy things that help your model find relevant information). You need multiple layers of safeguards (because let's face it, these models can get... creative). And you need monitoring systems that can actually understand natural language (turns out "the system is working fine" means something very different when language is involved). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8b9c3xadx2tk2ezvhfz4x2))
- And that is just the beginning. Take latency, for example. In some contexts (mobile) your channel architects expect responses in under 500ms, and for good reason. That's what users expect from a mobile app. But your GenAI service sometimes takes multiple seconds. This isn't just a technical issue; it completely changes how users experience the system and requires rethinking entire interaction patterns. It took a lot of collaboration with our architects to find the right balance, understanding that waiting seconds for an answer was a necessary trade-off in this new world. After all, long latency can destroy user experience faster than a bad model. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8b9n7z6v9afm6gj0h0pqjh))
- It's not just about making an API call, it's about building a robust system that can handle the unpredictability of language models while maintaining performance and reliability. These integration challenges fundamentally changed our approach to architecture. While we had always invested in AI architecture capabilities, the unique demands of GenAI led us to create a dedicated AI Architecture unit, a recognition that we were dealing with a different kind of complexity altogether. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8b9rdg34s0xg96wctsa2fz))
- A GenAI system might look perfect in testing but good luck proving it works consistently. Those standardized LLM benchmarks you see online? Largely irrelevant for real business applications. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8b9vyj76napyzw42fp6kva))
- I'll go further: specially when dealing with customer facing applications, building proper evaluation frameworks is possibly the most critical and challenging aspect of GenAI projects. In fact, I would argue that the number one priority of any team building a GenAI solution should be building the right tests. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8ba6xzvr8typke57n8e9fm))
- Here's why it's so problematic:
  • Traditional metrics just don't work here: there's no AUC or RMSE that can tell you if your response is actually good.
  • You're dealing with an almost infinite input space: testing every possible scenario is impossible.
  • Success means balancing technical accuracy with business appropriateness.
  • Edge cases are harder to spot and even harder to guard against.
  • Small changes in input can lead to dramatically different outputs. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8baeykmc3m5438sssqfwvq))
- This last point is particularly maddening. A slight change in how you phrase a prompt, adding a comma here, changing a word there, can dramatically alter the model's performance. As Ethan Mollick [says](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww%2Eoneusefulthing%2Eorg%2Fp%2Fa-guide-to-prompting-ai-for-what&urlhash=mQ32&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block), we may call it "prompt engineering," but it feels more like crafting magical incantations. Add these exact words, in this exact order, and the spell works perfectly. Change one word, and who knows what you'll summon? ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8bagqeezhrwrtj2nztfvah))
- We've made a lot of progress using LLM-as-a-Judge schemas (which basically uses an LLM to evaluate another LLM) but at a certain point you cannot escape the fact that you have to revise the answers manually by a human. So yeah, labeling is back on the menu (although to be fair, the labeling costs are not what they used to be). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8banan78gthv76e15ggvj1))
- With traditional AI, we've gotten pretty good at understanding risks. When a fraud model slips up or a recommendation system misfires, we can usually predict the impact. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8baxwnskcq0b3mn1ggrwzd))
- The questions keeping us up at night are way different now:
  • Just how wrong can these things get? (Spoiler: in surprisingly creative ways).
  • What if it gets chatty with users in... let's say, colorful language?
  • Ever had a model sound absolutely certain about something completely wrong?
  • Will it maintain a consistent personality across conversations?
  • What happens when it starts spilling secrets it hallucinated?
  • And my personal favorite: will users realize when the model is just making stuff up (and if not, how do we fail gracefully). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8bba4gx97g9v6teyw6316e))
- Building guardrails isn't just an add-on. It's becoming its own discipline. We've had to develop new types of monitoring systems and safety checks that go far beyond traditional model monitoring. And just when you think you've got it figured out, the base models improve and incorporate some of your safeguards, forcing you to rethink your approach. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8bbd3k6qpc2r4zry6th1s4))
- Here's a term we've coined: the "Regrettability Factor": the likelihood you'll regret starting a project in 6-9 months because the technology landscape has shifted so dramatically. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8bbnz1x5fpnztb86ngkrr9))
- ere's some of the stuff that we had to deal with:
  • We spent months building custom guardrails... only to see them show up in the next model release.
  • We spent quite a bit of time developing custom integration patterns. Then LangChain came along and made it all look like child's play.
  • Our fancy prompting system? Obsolete before we even finished documenting it.
  • We outright had to scrap several initiatives after realizing the market would provide similar solutions. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8bc6ka8q0ms3e3xhbd80gp))
- The 2-3 year planning horizon for traditional projects has compressed to 6-9 months with GenAI. This forces us to constantly ask: "What's our unique value add here?" and "What's the likelihood this becomes a commodity feature in the next few months?" ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8bcb14pcn4v2np8r587dt0))
- When everything changes so rapidly, the natural instinct is to wait for the dust to settle before making any moves. After all, why build something today that might be obsolete tomorrow? But that would be a mistake. A cautious approach is often riskier than making informed bets about where things are heading. We learned this firsthand: our early bet on Language AI in 2022 wasn't perfect timing (we wish we'd started even earlier), but having that foundation in place proved invaluable when GenAI exploded. In fact, the key to navigating this landscape is Anticipation. Yes, you'll get it wrong sometimes (we certainly have), but the cost of being wrong is often lower than the cost of being late or doing nothing. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8bcj2cmr19xt1nkdjk7jvk))
- The biggest plot twist in our GenAI journey is not the technology's capabilities (though they're mind-bending), or its limitations (though they're humbling). It's that with all this revolutionary power at our fingertips, we spend more time than ever sweating the basics. It's not what any of us expected, but it's where we find ourselves. Success still hinges on the fundamentals we've been discussing throughout this series. If anything, they're more critical now. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8bcv3khjwfs5htxahb7src))
- Think about it this way: when you hand someone a bicycle, you focus on teaching them how to balance. Hand them a motorcycle, and suddenly balance becomes even more crucial, not less. The stakes are just higher. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8bcx883zaaea62pczc2grv))
- The same applies to everything we've discussed in these posts. Worried about building the wrong thing? GenAI gives you a hundred new ways to build the wrong thing, faster and more impressively than ever before. Think integration is crucial? Try integrating a system that can say literally anything to your customers. Believe communication with stakeholders matters? Try explaining to them why your chatbot can write brilliantly one minute and completely miss the mark the next. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8bd09ctb3t3p2fehddx5k6))
- The power of these new models is seductive. They make everything seem possible. But possibility isn't the same as utility, and utility isn't the same as value. If I've learned anything in these past 24 months, it's that the hard parts of building AI products haven't gone away, they've just shifted. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8bd5kjn5kftbqharpabjk4))
- The technical barriers might be, in a sense, lower (no need to train your own billion-parameter model), but the strategic barriers are higher than ever. What should you build versus buy? How do you evaluate success? When do you trust the model, and when do you add guardrails? These aren't just technical decisions anymore: they're fundamental business strategy questions. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8bd8vsz59v80mcp9nq75wc))
- As for BBVA's journey with GenAI? We're still in the middle of it. Some of our assumptions have proven right, others hilariously wrong. The only thing I'm certain about is that by the time you read this, something fundamental will have changed in the GenAI landscape. And that's exactly why focusing on the fundamentals matters more than ever. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp8bdex7nmw5a4a99w0y9m9w))
