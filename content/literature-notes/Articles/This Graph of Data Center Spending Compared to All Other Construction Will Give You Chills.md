## Highlights
- Against all odds, the US economy continues to grow at a record-setting pace. Yet as new economic data shows, that growth is anything but healthy, and predicated almost entirely on the AI boom. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m4rh4dyqztwvmbj7ymvtsfsv))
- A [new essay](https://www.derekthompson.org/p/the-state-of-ai-in-2026in-26-charts) by podcaster and writer Derek Thompson takes a look at key economic metrics surrounding AI in 2026. Among the most striking is a bar graph showing growth in construction spending, a [key indicator](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesbusinesscouncil/2023/08/16/using-construction-as-an-economic-indicator/) of economic stability. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m4rh55p545zahcmprnpr5cxp))
- Using data from Harvard Kennedy School economist Jason Furman, Thompson shows that growth in data center construction has exploded this year, while all but one other category has regressed. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m4rh58skgkzdbq5w89re8gvn))
- By the numbers, data center construction has grown at a rate of 64.5 percent in the most recent year, while new energy infrastructure has grown by 4.1 percent to keep pace — since demand is growing for electricity to power the AI boom. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m4rh5b0dct6111g7rx7h4pfa))
- Everything else has collapsed: multi-family home building fell by 5.6 percent, while healthcare and commercial retail construction both tumbled by 10.1 percent. New educational construction tumbled by 12.6 percent, while manufacturing — supposedly a key target for the Trump administration — has collapsed, shrinking nearly 24 percent from 2025 to 2026. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m4rh5ct90q2hryq5h6xbz523))

- Thompson is perhaps best known for his “abundance” theory, a deregulation-brained [policy agenda](https://www.bostonreview.net/articles/the-real-path-to-abundance/) with roots in [neoliberal](https://jacobin.com/2024/09/neoliberalism-pandemic-third-way-solidarity) economic thought. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m4rh5n73d0yxamhejxvn2byq))
- But if there’s any abundance to be had in the US economy at the moment, it’s clearly coming on the back of AI — and with investors growing increasingly skeptical over the sustainability of AI spending, it could only be a matter of time before data center spending tumbles back down to Earth. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m4rh5p5v5ay7kxzj5pgfkrr2))
