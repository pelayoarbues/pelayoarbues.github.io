## Highlights
- Months after satellites picked up a [massive nuclear fusion facility](https://futurism.com/satellite-china-fusion-facility) in China's Sichuan province, the country's nuclear industry has blown the lid off fission tech. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jscxctkn7xzasvyz4z9x7hh3))
- During a private meeting earlier this month, researchers at the Chinese Academy of Sciences revealed the successful operation of a thorium-powered nuclear reactor located in the Gobi Desert. The team had achieved "full-power operation" last June, according to [*South China Morning Post*](https://www.scmp.com/?module=masthead&pgtype=article), and recently succeeded in reloading the reactor while it was powered up — a world first. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jscxczqjnvdjkvcbwjvv6m07))
- It's a major milestone for nuclear power. Thorium offers a more accessible but less weaponizable alternative to uranium, [according to the World Nuclear Association](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/current-and-future-generation/thorium), which notes that "thorium-based power reactor fuels would be a poor source for fissile material usable in the illicit manufacture of an explosive device." ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jscxd7t04ajk2pa2sqj9apdn))
- The Gobi Desert reactor is a two megawatt research unit engineered to use molten salt as fuel carrier and coolant. A [molten salt reactor](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/current-and-future-generation/molten-salt-reactors) (MSR) theoretically carries far less risk in the event of a meltdown compared to water-based systems, as salts can carry greater loads of thermal energy at much lower pressure. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jscxdj3rgfxqnnen2g24mqa0))
- In fact, a "meltdown" is basically a non-factor for these systems — the fuel is already molten. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jscxdtb0awy6q0fep7sx852y))
- A [report sponsored by the US government](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/469120#:~:text=A%20melt%20down%20is%20not,features%20reduce%20the%20accident%20plethora.) on MSRs notes that a "possible advantage of the MSR is that the fuel is subject to freezing," so "upon breach of a vessel or pipe... the fuel will disperse, and thus increase its cooling geometry, until it reaches a freezing configuration and thus will be confined to that location and configuration." Basically, imagine lava rolling slowly down a mountain as the air cools it back into rock, compared to a [spectacular steam explosion](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/appendices/chernobyl-accident-appendix-1-sequence-of-events) like the incident at Chernobyl. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jscxe31y2ddqtqpka17rkwyb))
- Curiously, MSRs are nothing new. They had their day in the US back in the late 1940s and early 50s, when American cold warriors dumped nearly $1 billion into developing a [nuclear-powered stealth bomber](https://thebulletin.org/2022/06/molten-salt-reactors-were-trouble-in-the-1960s-and-they-remain-trouble-today/). Congress [halted research](https://nuke.fas.org/space/anp-gao1963.pdf) on **t**horium-fueled airplanes back in 1961, and uranium more or less became the gold standard, due in no small part to its [military potential](https://fas.org/publication/uranium-mining-u-s-nuclear-weapons-program-3/). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jscxeg316txyssw122977k0c))
- Assumed obsolete, the US' MSR research has since been made public, forming the foundation of the Gobi Desert team's work. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jscxevht1xw9jzd8bka5t870))
- "The US left its research publicly available, waiting for the right successor," said the project's [chief scientist Xu Hongjie](https://interestingengineering.com/energy/china-builds-world-first-thorium-reactor). "Rabbits sometimes make mistakes or grow lazy. That’s when the tortoise seizes its chance." ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jscxf2mydw5jnwvtxts4egx7))
